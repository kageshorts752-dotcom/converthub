Which format should you pick?

Researched 8 Nov 2026 from YouTube Help, MDN, Kitaboo, SnipSound and Cloudinary (sources linked under each table). The one rule behind almost every answer: keep a high-quality master, and convert from that master — never convert lossy → lossy.

Still unconfirmed — check before you lock presets

  • No universal file-size limit exists for converter/SaaS uploads — published examples (32 MB free / 64 MB starter at one SaaS; 20 MB default / 100 MB max at another) are individual products' choices, not standards.
  • Meta publishes no bitrate for organic Reels — the 10–15 Mbps figures in guides are export advice, not a Meta spec. Reels length also differs between the app (up to 20 min) and the publishing API (15 min).
  • Safari AV1/WebM support depends on hardware and OS version — that's why the MP4/H.264 fallback matters. Test on a real iPhone rather than trusting a support table.
  • Platform specs change without notice — re-check YouTube Help / Meta / MDN before publishing encoder presets.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers — including the limits of converting in a browser.

Are my files uploaded to a server?

No. Every tool on this page runs in your browser using its built-in media and PDF engines, so your files never leave your device. That's also why there's no sign-up and no queue.

Which formats can I convert?

Images: JPG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, BMP, GIF and HEIC (where your browser can decode it) — out to JPG, PNG or WebP, plus PDF. PDF: to JPG/PNG images, Word (.doc), plain text, compressed PDF, merged and rotated. Audio: MP3, WAV, M4A, OGG, FLAC and Opus in — MP3 or WAV out. Video: MP4, MOV, WebM and more in — WebM out, or MP3/WAV audio extracted. Browsers can't decode MKV, AVI or WMV natively, so for those, a desktop tool like HandBrake or FFmpeg is the honest recommendation.

Why does video conversion take as long as the video itself?

Browser video conversion re-records the video in real time while it plays, using the same MediaRecorder engine browsers use for screen recording. A 2-minute clip takes about 2 minutes. Image, PDF and audio conversions are near-instant by comparison.

How do the exact-KB tools work?

They re-encode your image (or image-to-PDF) again and again, lowering quality — and, if needed, dimensions — until the file fits under the target, then show you the final size before you download. Form uploads (50 KB, 100 KB, 200 KB) are the most common use.

Will converting reduce quality?

Lossy formats (MP3, JPG, AAC) discard data every time they're re-encoded, so converting lossy → lossy (like MP3 → AAC) always costs a little quality. Convert from the highest-quality original you have — ideally a WAV/FLAC master for audio or the original photo for images. Source: SnipSound

What file size should I set as my upload limit?

There is no universal standard — it's an open question in our research. Observed SaaS examples range from 20–32 MB on free tiers to 2 GB per file on Teachable for course video. Pick limits from your storage and transcoding budget, and state them in the upload box.

What's the best format for a YouTube Short or Instagram Reel?

The same file works for both: MP4 with H.264 video and AAC-LC audio, 1080×1920 (9:16), 30 fps, exported at 8–15 Mbps, with the moov atom at the front (“fast start”). Shorts are capped at 1080p, so skip the 4K export. Note Meta doesn't publish an official bitrate for organic Reels — treat 10–15 Mbps as export advice, not a spec. Sources: YouTube Help, Monty
